# Kanban

Kanban is a framework that focuses on a consistent flow of work. Rather than committing to a set of work at the start of a sprint, the team pulls the next item from the backlog whenever it has capacity. This gives teams more flexible planning, clearer focus, and transparency throughout the development cycle, and it suits continuous deployment.

Useful measures for a Kanban team are throughput, lead time, and cycle time.

## The Kanban Board

Work items are shown on a kanban board, so anyone can see the state of every piece of work at any time. Columns represent the stages of the workflow, such as _To Do_, _In Progress_, and _Done_, and tickets move across as the work progresses. See [Work Tracking](1-ways-of-working/1.2-work-tracking.md) for how to keep a board up to date.

## Work-in-progress

Work in progress (WIP) limits set the maximum amount of work that can be in each stage of the workflow. Limiting WIP reduces multitasking and makes bottlenecks visible before they become serious, so the team finishes work instead of starting more.

> Too much WIP limits our ability to proactively put oneself in a position to prepare. Preparedness brings confidence to shape the future - rather than waiting to see what happens.
>
> _- Dominica DeGrandis_

## Goals and challenges

1. **Keep items small.** Aim for stories of about a day of work (see [Sizing](1-ways-of-working/1.1.5-sizing.md)). Small items flow through the board predictably and make bottlenecks easier to spot.

2. **Map WIP limits to the team's skills.** If a team has specialists, WIP limits may differ where they're involved. If bottlenecks occur, use the opportunity to teach other team members those skills and increase flow across the whole team.

3. **Reduce idleness.** When a team member has some downtime, encourage them to help an upstream or downstream team member. They'll contribute to the team, and learn something along the way.

4. **Protect a sustainable engineering culture.** WIP limits don't mean rushing work through a stage. They support solid engineering practices that protect the quality of the product and the health of the code base.
