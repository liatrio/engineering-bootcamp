# Agile

The Agile mindset is focused on collaboration and the ability to adapt to change quickly while focusing on delivering value to the customer. Agile utilizes an iterative approach that allows teams to respond to changing priorities and customer needs by working in small increments.

![Three Agile sprints each containing intermediate steps of design, build, test, review, and launch](img/devops-iterative.svg ':size=580x160 :class=img-center')

>Users don't know what they want until they see working software. - Humphrey's Law

## From Big Releases to Small Iterations

Traditionally, software projects ran on long release cycles, with requirements, design, build, test, and release handled as separate phases, often by separate groups. Agile breaks that work into short cycles. Each cycle produces working software, so the team gets feedback from users and stakeholders after every increment instead of at the end of the project.

## Agile Frameworks

Agile is a mindset, described in the [Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/), and many frameworks put it into practice. The two most widely used are:

- **[Scrum](1-ways-of-working/1.3.1-scrum.md)** organizes work into fixed-length sprints, with defined accountabilities and events. It's defined by the [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html).
- **[Kanban](1-ways-of-working/1.3.2-kanban.md)** optimizes the flow of work: it visualizes the workflow, limits work in progress, and improves the flow over time. It's defined by the [Kanban Guide](https://kanbanguides.org/english/).

Many teams blend the two. The rest of this section covers practices that teams commonly add to either framework: writing [stories](1-ways-of-working/1.3.3-stories.md), [scoping and refining](1-ways-of-working/1.3.4-scoping-and-refinement.md) them, [sizing](1-ways-of-working/1.3.5-sizing.md) them, and [tracking](1-ways-of-working/1.3.6-work-tracking.md) them. Neither guide requires these practices.
