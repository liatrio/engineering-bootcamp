# Kanban

The [Kanban Guide](https://kanbanguides.org/english/) defines Kanban as a strategy for optimizing the flow of value through a process. It has three practices:

- Defining and visualizing a workflow
- Actively managing items in a workflow
- Improving a workflow

Kanban has no sprints. Instead of committing to a set of work up front, the team pulls the next item whenever it has capacity. That makes planning flexible and suits continuous deployment.

## Flow Metrics

The Kanban Guide has four mandatory flow metrics:

- **WIP:** the number of items started but not finished.
- **Throughput:** the number of items finished per unit of time.
- **Work Item Age:** how long an unfinished item has been in progress.
- **Cycle Time:** how long an item took from start to finish.

Kanban doesn't require estimates. Instead, a team sets a **service level expectation (SLE)** from its historical cycle time, such as "85% of items will be finished in eight days or less," and uses it to forecast.

## The Kanban Board

Work items are shown on a kanban board, so anyone can see the state of every piece of work at any time. Columns represent the stages of the workflow, such as _To Do_, _In Progress_, and _Done_, and tickets move across as the work progresses. See [Work Tracking](1-ways-of-working/1.3.6-work-tracking.md) for how to keep a board up to date.

## Work-in-progress

Work in progress (WIP) limits set the maximum amount of work that can be in each stage of the workflow. Limiting WIP reduces multitasking and makes bottlenecks visible before they become serious, so the team finishes work instead of starting more.

> Too much WIP limits our ability to proactively put oneself in a position to prepare. Preparedness brings confidence to shape the future - rather than waiting to see what happens.
>
> _- Dominica DeGrandis_

## Goals and challenges

1. **Keep items small.** Small items flow through the workflow predictably, are more likely to meet the SLE, and make bottlenecks easier to spot. Watch Work Item Age to catch items that are getting stuck.

2. **Map WIP limits to the team's skills.** If a team has specialists, WIP limits may differ where they're involved. If bottlenecks occur, use the opportunity to teach other team members those skills and increase flow across the whole team.

3. **Reduce idleness.** When a team member has some downtime, encourage them to help an upstream or downstream team member. They'll contribute to the team, and learn something along the way.

4. **Protect a sustainable engineering culture.** WIP limits don't mean rushing work through a stage. They support solid engineering practices that protect the quality of the product and the health of the code base.
